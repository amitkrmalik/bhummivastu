It is 11:30 at night. You are tired after a long day, but your phone is charging beside your pillow, the television is on standby, and your laptop is still open on the bed. You want to sleep, but your mind keeps returning to messages, work, and tomorrow’s tasks.

Many bedrooms have slowly become charging stations, entertainment rooms, and work areas. Vastu Shastra offers a simple question to begin with: does the bedroom feel like a place for rest, or does it still feel active after the day is over?

## A home consultation: when the bedroom stays switched on

During a home Vastu consultation, a client described difficulty settling down at night. The bedroom contained a phone beside the pillow, a laptop charger near the bed, a television facing the sleeping area, and a drawer filled with old cables and unused devices.

The observation was not that technology was harmful or that one device had caused a health problem. The room simply had too many reminders of work, entertainment, and unfinished tasks. It was difficult for the space to feel quiet and complete at bedtime.

In traditional Vastu, the bedroom is generally reviewed as a space for rest, stability, and recovery. A room filled with active screens, indicator lights, wires, and notifications can feel unsettled, even before any directional interpretation is applied.

## Four areas to review in your bedroom

### 1. The phone and charger near the pillow

Keeping a phone beside the pillow makes it easy to check messages, news, and social media whenever you wake. The light, alerts, and habit of reaching for the phone can keep the day mentally active.

Try charging the phone on a table away from the bed. If you need it for an alarm, use Do Not Disturb or airplane mode and keep the screen facing away from you.

### 2. The television facing the bed

A television directly in front of the bed can make the bedroom feel like an entertainment room rather than a resting space. Even when switched off, a bright standby light or reflective screen may draw attention.

If the television must remain in the room, switch it off completely before sleep and cover the screen with a light, opaque cloth. Keep the volume, notifications, and remote controls away from the bedside where possible.

### 3. Tangled wires and unused devices

Old chargers, broken phones, spare cables, and unused power adapters often collect in drawers or corners. They are easy to ignore because they are small, but they make the room feel unfinished.

Sort the cables once a month. Recycle broken electronics responsibly, return usable items to their proper storage, and keep active charging wires neatly arranged. If you follow directional Vastu principles, keep the North and North-East portions of the room light, clear, and free from unnecessary electronic clutter.

### 4. Laptops and work reminders near the bed

When a laptop, notebook, or work bag remains open beside the bed, it can be harder to create a clear boundary between working hours and rest. A simple basket or shelf outside the sleeping area can keep work items together without making the room feel like an office.

## A simple electronic reset for the bedroom

You do not need an expensive renovation. Try these small changes:

- Finish screen-based work at least 30 minutes before bedtime when possible.
- Charge phones and laptops away from the pillow and sleeping side of the bed.
- Switch off televisions, consoles, and unnecessary standby devices.
- Keep the bedroom cool, quiet, dark, and comfortable.
- Use cable organisers instead of allowing wires to spread across the floor.
- Keep broken gadgets and unopened packages out of the bedroom.
- Use the final part of the evening for a quiet routine such as reading, prayer, gentle stretching, or conversation.

These steps support a calmer bedroom and also make the Vastu goal of a balanced resting space easier to follow. No salt bowl, plant, or special object can replace the simple work of reducing clutter and changing the bedtime routine.

## What to observe for one week

After making the changes, notice practical details rather than looking for an instant miracle. Is it easier to stop checking the phone? Does the room feel quieter? Are you spending less time working from bed? Do you wake up feeling more settled?

Sleep is affected by many factors, including stress, caffeine, routine, light, noise, and health. If you continue to have significant difficulty sleeping or feel unwell during the day, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

## A five-minute bedroom check

Before going to bed tonight, look around the room and ask:

- Is a phone or laptop charging beside my head?
- Is the television or another screen facing the bed?
- Are unused wires or damaged devices taking up space?
- Can I switch off work notifications before sleeping?
- Does the room feel calm enough for rest?

Start with one change: move the charger away from the pillow and switch off the screen. A bedroom does not need to be free of every modern device, but it should have a clear transition from activity to rest.
