A bedroom is meant to help two people slow down together. But in many homes, the television faces the bed, phones charge beside the pillows, and conversations continue while one person is still watching a show or checking messages.

The goal is not to blame technology. It is to ask whether the bedroom supports rest, connection, and a calm end to the day.

## A home consultation: when the television became the third presence

During a home Vastu consultation, a couple described a familiar pattern. They watched television in bed most nights, kept their phones on the bedside tables, and often fell asleep with the screen on standby. The room was comfortable, but it remained active long after the rest of the home had become quiet.

The television was directly opposite the bed. Its dark screen reflected light from the room, and notifications from the phones interrupted conversations. The observation was not that the screen had caused conflict in the relationship. It was that the bedroom had very few signals that the day was over.

In traditional Vastu, the bedroom is generally reviewed as a place for stability, privacy, and rest. Many practitioners prefer to keep large active screens and reflective surfaces away from the direct view of the bed.

## Why TV placement matters in a shared bedroom

A television facing the bed can encourage late-night viewing and keep the eyes and mind engaged. Even when switched off, a reflective screen may remain visually noticeable.

If possible, move the television to a shared living area. This gives the bedroom a clearer purpose and makes it easier for both people to agree on a bedtime routine.

If the television must remain in the bedroom:

- switch it off fully instead of leaving it on standby
- cover the screen with a light, opaque cloth before sleep
- keep the remote and streaming devices away from the pillows
- reduce the volume and screen brightness in the final part of the evening
- avoid using the television as background noise throughout the night

These are simple changes, not promises of a relationship transformation.

## Keep the shared sleeping area stable

Vastu reviews the bedroom as a whole, not just the television wall. Look at the bed, the headboard, the bedside tables, storage, mirrors, and electronic devices.

Where the layout permits, keep the bed supported by a solid wall and leave enough space for both people to enter and exit comfortably. Avoid allowing work files, exercise equipment, laundry, or unused boxes to take over the room.

The South-West portion of a bedroom is traditionally associated with stability in many Vastu approaches. If it is used for the primary bedroom, keep it grounded and orderly rather than filling it with constantly active screens or unnecessary clutter. A complete floor-plan review is more useful than applying one direction as a fixed rule.

## Create a shared electronic boundary

Small technology habits can make the bedroom feel more peaceful:

- Charge phones away from the pillow and use Do Not Disturb at night.
- Finish work messages and intense screen use before getting into bed.
- Keep laptops and work bags outside the sleeping area where possible.
- Set a shared screen-free period, even if it begins with only 30 minutes.
- Replace late-night scrolling with reading, prayer, stretching, or conversation.
- Keep the room quiet, comfortable, and dim before sleep.

The most important part is agreement. A bedroom reset works better when both people choose the same boundary rather than one person trying to enforce it alone.

## A five-minute couple’s bedroom check

Walk into the bedroom together and ask:

- Is the television the first thing we see from the bed?
- Are our phones and chargers within easy reach all night?
- Do work and entertainment continue after we lie down?
- Can both of us move around the bed comfortably?
- Does the room feel private, quiet, and ready for rest?

Choose one change that both people can maintain. Cover the screen, move the chargers, or create a short screen-free routine before making larger changes.

## A calmer shared space

Vastu can offer a helpful framework for reviewing the balance of a bedroom, but it cannot replace communication, kindness, or professional relationship support. A screen does not create an argument by itself; habits, stress, and unresolved conversations also matter.

Start with the physical environment. Keep the television out of the direct line of sight where possible, reduce bedside electronics, and give the bedroom a clear transition from activity to rest. A calmer room can make it easier for both people to reconnect at the end of the day.
