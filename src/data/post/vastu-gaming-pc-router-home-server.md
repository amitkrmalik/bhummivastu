Many homes now contain more technology than a small office. A gaming PC may run for hours, a router stays connected all day, and a home server or network-attached storage device may operate through the night.

These devices are useful, but they also bring heat, fan noise, indicator lights, cables, and constant activity. Vastu offers a way to review where this technology sits, while practical requirements such as ventilation, power, and connectivity still come first.

## A home consultation: when the study corner became a tech zone

During a home Vastu consultation, a client showed us a gaming and home-server setup placed in the North-East corner of a study room. The setup included a large desktop tower, a UPS, two monitors, a router, and several loose cables.

The equipment worked well, but the corner had become warm and visually crowded. The client also used the room for reading and planning, yet the screens, lights, and fan noise made it difficult for the space to feel settled when the equipment was running.

The observation was not that the devices had automatically caused a money or career problem. The useful question was whether the home could place active, heat-producing technology in a more suitable zone without reducing performance or safety.

## How Vastu looks at active technology

In many Vastu practices, the South-East is reviewed for fire, electrical activity, and heat. The North-East is commonly kept light, clear, and open. This creates a practical starting point:

- review the South-East for large heat-producing equipment when the layout permits
- keep the North-East free from unnecessary weight and cable clutter
- use the South or West portions of a room for heavier storage when appropriate
- keep sleeping areas separate from active work and gaming equipment where possible

These are traditional space-planning associations, not guarantees of wealth, focus, or health. The best location also depends on network coverage, cooling, noise, power access, and how the family uses the room.

## Gaming PC: manage heat, light, and long sessions

A gaming PC is often used for long periods and may generate noticeable heat. Place it where air can circulate freely, keep vents clear, and avoid pushing the tower into a closed cabinet.

If the gaming setup is in the North-East, consider whether it can move toward the South-East or another suitable active zone. If it must remain there, reduce the visual load around it, organise the cables, and keep the rest of the North-East corner open.

Create a clear boundary between gaming and sleep. Turn off bright lighting, close the session at a planned time, and avoid allowing the equipment to become the centre of a room that is also used for quiet study or rest.

## Router: prioritise coverage and calm placement

A router needs a practical location for reliable coverage. It does not need to be treated as a harmful object simply because it is electronic.

Keep it on a stable, ventilated surface rather than inside a tightly closed box. Avoid placing it beside the bed if lights, alerts, or the habit of checking the network disturb sleep. Where coverage allows, place it in a shared living or work area instead of making the bedroom the home’s technology hub.

## Home server and storage: review weight and access

A home server, UPS, or network-storage unit may be heavier than a normal computer and may run continuously. Keep the equipment accessible for maintenance, away from damp areas, and positioned so that heat does not collect around it.

From a Vastu perspective, review whether the North-East is carrying the heaviest part of the setup. If the equipment can move toward the South-East or a stable South or West area without affecting the network, that may create a more balanced arrangement.

Do not hide active equipment behind curtains, inside crowded cupboards, or under piles of storage. A tidy appearance is useful only when the setup can still breathe and be maintained properly.

## Five-minute technology audit

Walk around your home and ask:

- Is the heaviest electronic setup in the North-East?
- Is heat collecting around a gaming PC, UPS, or server?
- Are cables crossing the floor or blocking a path?
- Is the router or screen activity disturbing a bedroom?
- Can the equipment move without reducing coverage or safe operation?

Make one change at a time. Clear the cables first, improve airflow next, and then review whether the equipment belongs in the room and direction where it currently sits.

## A balanced home setup

Technology is part of modern life, not something to fear. Vastu is most useful here as a prompt to balance active equipment with quiet, open areas of the home.

Keep the North-East light where possible, review the South-East for heat-producing technology, and choose locations that support safe, comfortable everyday use. A well-planned setup should work for both the household and the devices inside it.
