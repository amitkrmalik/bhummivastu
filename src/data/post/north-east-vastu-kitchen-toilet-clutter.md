The North-East corner of a home often attracts strong opinions. Some people call it the most important part of the house, while others worry that one room or object in this direction will bring immediate problems.

A calmer approach is more useful. In traditional Vastu, the North-East—often called **Ishanya**—is generally kept light, open, clean, and peaceful. If a kitchen, toilet, heavy storage, or everyday clutter occupies this zone, review the complete layout before making a decision.

## A home consultation: when the North-East feels overloaded

During a home Vastu consultation, a family shared that the North-East corner had gradually become a utility area. A kitchen counter held several appliances and grocery cartons. Cleaning supplies were stacked nearby, and the passage beside the bathroom door was difficult to keep dry and clear.

The observation was not that these features had automatically caused a health, money, or relationship problem. The practical issue was that the most open part of the home had become warm, crowded, and difficult to maintain.

The first recommendation was to understand the entire floor plan and then reduce the load on the zone wherever possible. That approach is more helpful than creating fear about one room or promising a single instant remedy.

## 1. Kitchen or cooking activity in the North-East

Traditional Vastu commonly reviews the North-East separately from the South-East, which is often associated with cooking and fire. A kitchen in the North-East brings heat, movement, appliances, food storage, and daily activity into a zone that many traditions prefer to keep light.

If the kitchen cannot be moved, begin with practical observations:

- Is the cooking area well ventilated?
- Are the stove, sink, and storage arranged safely?
- Are heavy appliances and large grocery cartons taking over the corner?
- Is the area clean, dry, and easy to use?

Where the layout permits, keep the heaviest storage toward the south or west portions of the kitchen and avoid filling the North-East corner with unused items. Any change to the cooking layout should still follow normal kitchen, electrical, and fire-safety requirements.

## 2. Toilet or bathroom in the North-East

Traditional Vastu often gives special attention to toilets and bathrooms in the North-East. The practical review should focus on moisture, ventilation, cleanliness, drainage, and the condition of the door—not on frightening predictions.

Check for leaking taps, damp walls, blocked exhaust fans, unpleasant odours, or a bathroom door that remains open toward the main living area. Repairing leaks, improving airflow, keeping the door closed when appropriate, and maintaining the space can make a noticeable difference to how the home feels.

If the bathroom is part of the original building plan, do not assume demolition is the only answer. A complete assessment can identify what can be improved through maintenance, usage, ventilation, and layout adjustments.

## 3. Heavy storage and unused items

Suitcases, unopened boxes, spare tiles, old appliances, and unused furniture can slowly collect in a corner. When the North-East becomes the default storage area, it may feel closed and difficult to approach.

Use a simple three-step check:

1. Remove items that are broken or no longer needed.
2. Move heavy, rarely used storage toward a more suitable part of the home if the layout allows.
3. Keep the North-East floor, windows, and walking path clear.

The goal is not to throw away useful belongings. It is to stop the entry, light, and movement of the home from being blocked by things that are not used every day.

## 4. Daily clutter and visual heaviness

Small items can create more visual pressure than one large cabinet. Shoes, delivery parcels, laundry baskets, school bags, wires, and cleaning tools are common examples.

Create a simple home for each category. Keep shoes together, move parcels away after they arrive, store cleaning products safely, and avoid allowing the corner to become a temporary dumping place. Light colours, steady lighting, and fresh air can support the feeling of openness, but no colour or object can replace regular care.

## Simple non-demolition steps

If the North-East needs attention, begin with changes that are easy to observe:

- Clear the floor and the walking path.
- Remove unused cartons and broken items.
- Repair leaks and improve ventilation.
- Reduce heavy appliances and storage in the corner where possible.
- Keep the area bright, clean, and easy to access.
- Use the space for quiet activities such as prayer, reading, or reflection if it suits the family.

Avoid applying a direction-specific object or remedy without understanding the complete floor plan. North-East, South-East, and South-West conditions affect one another in many Vastu approaches.

## A simple first step

Stand at the centre of the home with a reliable compass and identify the North-East zone. Take a photograph, list what is there, and note any heat, moisture, clutter, or blocked movement.

Then choose one practical change for the week. A cleaner, drier, lighter, and more usable North-East is a sensible starting point for any Vastu review.
